▣Defects in ceramics do not occur alone as electroneutrality must be maintained
▣Frenkel defect: 1 cation vacancy + 1 cation interstitial
▣Schottky defect: 1 cation vacancy + 1 anion vacancy

Abrasive Ceramics
2026-27 AMSC5720 - Prof. Y. Chen
12
▣Abrasive ceramics are particulate-form ceramics used to wear, grind, or cut 
away other softer materials.
▣Requirements: hardness (Mohs ≥ 7), toughness (resist fracture), 
refractoriness (withstand frictional heat)
▣Common types: 
▣Natural: diamond, corundum (aluminum oxide), emery (inpure
corundum), sand, calcite (calcium carbonate), etc.
▣Manufactured: diamond, corundum, borazon (cubic BN or CBN), 
carborundum (silicon carbide), ZrO₂–Al₂O₃, etc.
▣Superabrasives: extremely hard
▣Applications: grinding, polishing, lapping, drilling, cutting, sharpening, sanding.

Ceramic Biomaterials
2026-27 AMSC5720 - Prof. Y. Chen
14
▣Ceramic materials used in biomedical applications require properties include chemical 
inertness, hardness, wear resistance, and low coefficient of friction.
▣Ceramics typically used for implants include crystalline oxide materials, glasses, and 
glass-ceramics
▣Common cases
▣
High-purity and dense Al2O3 that is highly inert and unreactive:load-bearing orthopedic 
applications involving skeletal injuries (e.g., hip prostheses)
▣
Zirconia (tetragona form), partially stablized with yttria (i.e. Y-TZP): orthopedic and dental 
applications (e.g. femoral heads, dental crowns)
▣
Glass/glass ceramic coatings: promote bonding with tissues surrounding bones
▣
Dissolvable implants: graduate infiltrated with and replaced by natural bon tissue during 
healing (i.e. ultimately dissolves and becomes absorbed into the body of the host) 
Typical materials: tricalcium phosphate (Ca3PO4), hydroxyapatite (Ca10(PO4)6(OH)2
