Solar Cell Working Principle
General Procedure:
❑Light Absorption and Exciton Formation: Photons 
can be absorbed by a semiconductor material like 
silicon, The absorbed energy knocks electrons from 
their normal atomic bonds to excited states, 
creating electron-hole pairs, which is called 
exciton.
❑Charge Carrier Separation: A built-in electric field 
pushes the loosely paired electrons and holes to 
separate and move towards in opposite directions. 
❑Charge Carrier Collection: Electrons and holes 
eventually reach the corresponding electrodes, 
generating a current flow to power external load.

Charge Carrier Separation
A solar cell is a typical p-n junction diode
❑Depletion Region:
•
As electrons and holes diffuse, fixed charges are exposed (negative on the p-side, positive on 
the n-side). This creates an electric field that opposes further diffusion.
•
When the new equilibrium is formed, the electric field is called built-in potential (Vbi).
•
The distance that electrons and holes are separated is called depletion region width.  
❑p-n Junction Formation:
•
p-type: High hole concentration.
•
n-type: High electron concentration.
•
Diffusion Current: Charge carrier movement 
due to concentration difference. Electrons 
diffuse from n-type to p-type, and holes diffuse 
from p-type to n-type
•
Drift Current: Charge Carrier movement due 
to potential difference

Surface Recombination and Passivation
The defects at a semiconductor surface are caused by the interruption to the 
periodicity of the crystal lattice, which causes dangling bonds at the 
semiconductor surface. The reduction of the number of dangling bonds, and 
hence the recombination, is achieved by growing a layer on top of the 
semiconductor surface which ties up some of these dangling bonds. This 
reduction of dangling bonds in known as surface passivation.

Organic Photovoltaics (OPVs)
❖Organic photovoltaic (OPV) cells use donor (D) and acceptor (A) 
materials, not p-n junctions like inorganic cells. (Lecture 3)
❖Donor/Acceptor absorbs photons and generates excitons (electron-hole 
pairs). Donor/Acceptor receives holes/electrons after exciton 
dissociation.
Donor
Acceptor
❑Charge Carrier Conductivity:
Delocalization of π Electrons:
•
Overlapping π orbitals lead to extended electron mobility.
•
Charge carriers can move freely, enhancing conductivity.
HOMO and LUMO:
•
HOMO: Highest Occupied Molecular Orbital.
•
LUMO: Lowest Unoccupied Molecular Orbital.
❑Energy Gap:
1. Band gap (HOMO-LUMO gap) is typically 1–4 eV.
2. High absorption coefficient: ~10^5 cm⁻¹.
3. Allows visible light photons to excite electrons from HOMO to LUMO.

=== PAGE 32 ===
Working Mechanism of OPVs
❑Photon Absorption:
•
Photons with energy > band gap create excitons (bound electron-hole pairs).
❑Exciton Diffusion:
•
Excitons in OPVs are strongly bound due to the low dielectric constant of 
organic materials.
•
Exciton binding energy: ~0.3 eV, much higher than in inorganic materials.
•
Short diffusion length (~10 nm) is due to the localized nature of electrons in 
organic molecules.
❑Exciton Dissociation:
•
At the donor-acceptor interface, excitons split into free charges
•
The LUMO offset of donor and acceptor must be larger than exciton binding 
energy. 
❑Charge Transfer and Transport:
✓If excitons are generated from donors
•
Electrons transfer to acceptor domains and transport to cathode.
•
Holes remain in donor domains and transport to anode.
✓Vice versa

=== PAGE 33 ===
Bulk Heterojunctions-Basics 
❑Advantages:
•
Bulk heterojunctions outperform layered structures by allowing for 
thicker layers for effective photon absorption.
•
Simplifies processing compared to oriented layered structures 
while maintaining similar performance levels.
❑Structure of Bulk Heterojunctions:
•
Comprised of a nanoscale mixture of donor and acceptor materials.
•
Typically includes conjugated molecules as donors and fullerenes as acceptors.
❑Nanoscale Domain Size:
•
Aims to significantly increase interface area for light absorption 
and exciton dissociation.
•
Enables short-lived excitons to reach the interface.
•
Increased donor-acceptor interface area enhances exciton 
dissociation.
❑Challenges:
•
Requires control over domain size to form a percolating network.
•
Otherwise: Charges may become trapped in donor or acceptor-rich domains, leading to recombination.
