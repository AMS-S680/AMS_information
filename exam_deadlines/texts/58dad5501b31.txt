What is catalysis?

Catalyst – a substance that accelerates a chemical reaction, and it is not consumed.
For example, catalase (enzyme, biological catalyst) drives the reaction:

Photocatalysis
Depending on the mechanism how energy of electromagnetic radiation in UV-vis range of
electromagnetic spectrum is used by the photocatalyst to drive a reaction, we distinguish between:
(1) Photoredox catalysis. A photocatalyst (in this case photoredox catalyst) absorbs a photon forming
an electronically excited state, which mediates redox reactions of reactants. Vast majority of
photocatalytic reactions reported until now are photoredox catalytic reactions.
(2) Energy transfer (EnT). A photocatalyst (in this case often called as “sensitizer”) absorbs a photon
forming an electronically excited state followed by energy transfer to the reactant.
(3) Atom transfer. A photocatalyst (in this case atom transfer catalyst) absorbs a photon forming an
electronically excited state, which abstracts an atom (usually hydrogen atom) from reactants and pass
this atom to another substrate.

Photocatalysis
The mode of action (reaction mechanism) depends on chemical structure of the employed
photocatalyst and the reactants.
•
The same molecule can act as a photoredox catalyst or a sensitizer depending on the chemical
structure of a reactant – a “hand-and-glove” concept.

Application of this trend to tune HOMO-LUMO energy levels in a series of structurally-similar
