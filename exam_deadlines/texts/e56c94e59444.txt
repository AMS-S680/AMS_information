The basic Auger process starts with the removal of an inner shell electron, leaving a vacancy (hole). Several processes are capable of producing the hole, but bombardment with 
an electron beam 
is the most common one. The hole is filled by a second electron from an outer shell, with excess energy simultaneously released. A third electron, the Auger electron, escapes carrying the excess energy in a 
radiationless
 process. Such a process of an excited atom decaying into a doubly charged ion by ejection of electrons is called the Auger process.

=== PAGE 53 ===
Analysis Procedure
Conducting solids (metals, alloys, semiconductors, thin films) with dimensions less than 2 cm diameter and 1 cm high may be analyzed. Samples must be clean and free from any high vapor pressure material. It is important that the specimen surface should not be handled.
Limitations 
Samples must be conductive
, Samples must be solid and vacuum compatible.  Insulating samples can be more difficult.  Because Auger is a surface sensitive technique, gold or carbon coating must be avoided.
Qualitative detection sensitivity is reliable to ~1.0 atomic percent depending on the element
Beam damage possible with some materials
Accuracy of quantitative analysis is limited to ± 20% if published sensitivity factors are used, better quantification is possible if standards closely resembling the sample are used
Elements H and He are undetectable
