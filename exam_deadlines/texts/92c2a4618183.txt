What are the SEs?
[Speaker notes]
Secondary electrons are those generated by the primary electrons experience multiple elastic/inelastic  scattering.  Core electrons have higher probability to go through Auger process, but the valence electrons have much higher change to escape the work function and be collected.

=== PAGE 6 ===
Energy Distribution of Electrons Leaving the Sample Surface
Energy range of the Electrons
[Speaker notes]
Secondary electrons are generated by a primary electron beam (PE) that undergoes multiple inelastic scattering events. A single primary electron does not give up all its energy to one electron; instead, it causes multiple "gentle" ionizations, mostly affecting weakly bound valence electrons. 
Most "true" secondary electrons are produced within the bulk and must travel to the surface to escape. During this travel, they undergo frequent collisions, losing almost all their initial kinetic energy

Sampling Depth of BSEs
The sampling depth of BSEs is a substantial fraction of the electron range;
The BSE signal is not a surface-sensitive signal at conventional SEM beam energies (> 10 keV);
As the beam energy is decreased, the range decreases rapidly, and the BSE signal becomes much more surface-sensitive.
