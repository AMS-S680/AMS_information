2.1 Vapors and Gases
The behavior of vapors and gases on both the macroscopic and 
molecular scales is fundamental to an understanding of thin-film 
deposition from the vapor phase. We will examine the molecular 
interaction of gases and will relate that behavior to the macroscopic, 
measurable properties such as temperature and pressure.
The behaviors of vapors and gases need to be first distinguished. At a fixed 
temperature, T, a vapor can be condensed to a liquid or solid by compressing 
it sufficiently, whereas a gas cannot.

2.5 Units of Pressure Measurement
Many different pressure units are frequently used in thin-film 
technology either for historical reasons or for convenience of size.
1 torr = 133 Pa = 1 mmHg
1 bar = 750 torr = 1.0 105 Pa = 0.99 standard atmosphere (atm)
The standard conditions of T and p (stp) are 0 C and 1 atm (760 torr). At stp, 
the molecular density is n = 2.69 1019 molecules cm3. The molar volume is 
Vm = 22400 cm3 mol1.
The term usually seen in gas supply monitoring is the “mass” flow rate 
measured in standard cm3 per minute or standard cm3 per second or 
standard liters per minute (sccm, sccs, slm). 1 sccm = 4.48 1017
molecules s1. In vacuum pumping, mass flow rate (pump throughput) is 
often measured in torr l s1 or Pa l s1.
1 psi = 6894.757 Pa

2.6 Knudsen Equation
The Knudsen equation is one of the most important relationships in 
thin-film technology.
Consider M = 40 g mol1, T = 25 C, p = 0.001 Pa, Ji = 2.4 1015 molecules cm2 s1.
For a molecular diameter of 0.3 nm, there are 1.4 1015 molecules cm2 in a 
monolayer (ML, assume close packing and neglect empty interstitial areas). If all the 
molecules colliding on a surface stick on it in a thin-film deposition process, then the 
accumulation rate is 1.7 ML/s = 1.8 m/h.

This is a typical thin-film deposition rate. It also means that if this gas is a 
background impurity in the deposition chamber, the film is likely to incorporate a 
high level of contamination. To deposit a 99.9 % pure film at 1.8 m/h, the p of 
impurity gases must be kept below 106 Pa = 1011 atm, which is considered to be in 
the range of ultrahigh vacuum (UHV).
The impingement flux is determined by the partial p of each gases. The film 
purity is determined by the ratio between the film molecular impingement flux 
and the impurity impingement flux. The film purity can be improved by 
decreasing the impurity partial p value.
